Fixed risk per trade: why it matters more than the return percentage

When people choose a trading system, they almost always look at the return: "how many percent per month". It is the most visible number and the least useful one for a decision. A return means nothing without the risk per trade: two systems with the same annual result can differ several times over in their worst case — and the worst case is what decides whether the account survives the year.

Why "+30% a month" tells you nothing

A return is an outcome, not a property of the system. It depends on how many trades were taken, how much of the account was risked on each, and how lucky that particular stretch was. The same system gives +30% on one account and a loss on another, with position size as the only difference.

The useful question is different: what is the maximum loss built into a single trade, and what happens if trades go against you one after another.

What fixed risk per trade means

It is a share of the account defined in advance that the system may lose on one trade — and it does not change from trade to trade or with mood.

An example calculation:

  • account: $10,000
  • risk per trade: 1% = $100 maximum loss on a single trade
  • ten consecutive losses = −$1,000, which is −10% of the account

Now the same without fixed risk: position size is scaled "by conviction". Ten bad trades at 5–10% of the account each produce −50% to −100%: the account is finished. The difference is not forecasting skill — that is identical in both cases. The difference is that in the first case the loss size is known before entering, and in the second it is discovered afterwards.

Why this changes how a drawdown behaves

A drawdown is not a crash; it is a normal part of how a system works — some share of trades is losing by design. The only question is depth.

When the maximum loss per trade is fixed, the depth of a drawdown becomes predictable: it is the sum of however many losing trades arrive in a row, not of random position sizes. That is why a system can be discussed in terms of "how many consecutive losses it survives" rather than "what it returns in a good month".

What fixed risk does not solve

This part matters, so plainly:

  • It does not make a system profitable. Risk control caps the loss; it does not create an edge. A losing strategy with perfect risk control is still a losing strategy.
  • Risk per trade is not risk per streak. Ten trades at 1% are not 10% of guaranteed loss, and certainly not 1%: streaks happen more often than people expect.
  • It does not protect against gaps and slippage. On a market-open gap or significant slippage, the actual loss can exceed the calculated one. Fixed risk states an intention, not a physical limit.
  • It does not cancel drawdowns. An account with fixed risk can still spend months below its high.

How to check a system before committing money

  1. Ask what risk is built into a single trade and how it is capped technically.
  2. Look at the drawdown, not the return: how deep the account went and how long it took to recover.
  3. Ask for live results, not only a backtest. A backtest shows how the system behaved on past data; live results show how it behaves now.
  4. Verify on a demo or a minimal account before funding a working balance.
