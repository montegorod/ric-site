Commission out of profit only: why it is rare and how to verify it

Start with a sentence that explains the entire industry: most people in finance get paid whether you make money or not.

That is not a conspiracy, it is a business model. A manager has staff, data, software and rent to pay in a bad month too, so the standard arrangement charges you for the assets, not for the result. Our arrangement is the other one: commission out of profit only. It is rare, and it is worth understanding exactly how rare, and what it does not mean.

How managers usually charge

  • A management fee — a percentage of the money under management, charged monthly or quarterly regardless of how the period went.
  • A subscription — a fixed payment for access to signals or a strategy, again independent of the result.
  • A performance fee on top — a share of the profit, but added to the management fee rather than replacing it.
  • A markup you never see — a wider spread or a commission on each trade, collected through the broker.

Often these come in combination. The manager's income then barely depends on your outcome, and the incentive to protect your capital is weaker than the incentive to keep assets under management.

How our arrangement works

Our commission comes out of profit. There is no management fee, no subscription and no charge for effort. If a month closes flat or negative, we receive nothing.

The exact share is fixed in writing before you connect, along with how and when it is settled — depending on the broker, that happens automatically or manually, typically on a weekly basis. What matters more than the mechanics is the direction: no profit on your account, no payment to us.

What "only from profit" does not mean

It does not mean free. A share of profit is a real cost. In a good period it is larger than a management fee would have been — that is the deal. You are trading a fixed cost in bad times for a larger cost in good times.

It does not mean a good month is coming. A month without profit costs you nothing in commission, but it still costs you the month. Our earning nothing is our problem, not a signal about the market.

It does not remove the broker's costs. Spreads, swaps and per-trade commissions are charged by the broker on every trade, in every month, profitable or not. Those are not ours to waive.

It does not align us perfectly. Ask how profit is defined, how open positions are treated, and what happens if you withdraw in the middle of a period. A serious manager has a written answer for each of those; a salesman has a friendly one.

How to verify it before your money moves

Ask these questions and ask for the answers in writing.

  1. What do I pay in a month where the account ends lower? The right answer is: nothing.
  2. Profit net of what? Are the broker's costs and previous losses deducted before your share is calculated?
  3. If I lose money and then recover it, do I pay your share twice on the same ground? A high-water condition means no — and it is worth having in writing.
  4. When exactly is the commission taken, and by whom — you, or the broker automatically?
  5. What happens if I stop or withdraw mid-cycle, after a partial month?
  6. Where is all of this written down, and can I read it before I fund the account?

If a manager cannot answer these six plainly, the phrase "we only charge on profit" is marketing rather than a term of business.

What we do not promise

We do not claim that "no fee in a bad month" makes an investment safe, and we do not promise that a bad month will not happen. What we can state: 50% of profit only, settled weekly, no management fee and no subscription, and no payment to us in a week that produces no profit.
