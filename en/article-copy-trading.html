What is copy trading and how does it work?

Copy trading means an experienced trader's trades are automatically repeated on your account. They open a trade, and the same trade appears on your account at the same moment, scaled to your deposit. You don't have to watch charts, learn analysis or make decisions: the trader you chose does that. Below, in plain words: how copy trading works, how it differs from signals and managed accounts, where the catches are, and how to choose whom to copy.

How copy trading works, in plain words

There are two accounts: the trader's and yours. Both are opened with a broker, usually in the MetaTrader trading platform. Between them sits a copying service: it sees every trade the trader makes and repeats it on your account within a fraction of a second.

One important detail is proportion. If the trader's account is $10,000 and yours is $1,000, your trade will be ten times smaller. So your result in percent will be roughly the same as the trader's, not in dollars.

What happens to your money? Nothing: it stays in your account with the broker, opened in your name. The trader only gets the right to trade — to open and close positions. They cannot deposit or withdraw. This is the main difference between copy trading and schemes where you hand your money over "to be managed".

Copy trading vs signals vs managed accounts

These three are often confused, but they work differently.

Signals. A trader posts on Telegram: "buy gold at this price, stop-loss here". You open the trade yourself and decide whether to take part. It is a good option for those who want to trade on their own and learn from an experienced trader's trades. You just need to stay in touch: gold moves fast, and it is best to enter right after the signal.

Managed accounts and PAMM accounts. You transfer money to a manager or to a pooled account where they trade the money of many investors. Here you depend not only on their trading but also on their honesty: the money is physically not with you.

Copy trading. Trades open on their own, without your involvement and without delay, while the money stays in your account. In effect it combines the best of both: the automation of a managed account and the control over your money of trading yourself.

What you control in copy trading

  • Your money. The account is in your name with the broker. You deposit and withdraw yourself, at any time.
  • Every trade. All open positions and the full history are visible in your MetaTrader terminal in real time. Nothing happens "somewhere out there".
  • Stopping. Copying can be switched off. Whether to continue is always your decision.

Can you make money with copy trading, and what are the risks?

You can, but copy trading is not a bank deposit. You repeat the trading of a real trader, which means you get both their profitable and their losing periods. A week or a month can end in a loss, and that is a normal part of trading. It is fairer to judge results over six months to a year.

The main risks:

  • The trader themselves. If they trade without stop-losses or sharply increase position size after losses, sooner or later it ends in a deep drawdown. Check not the returns, but how they limit losses.
  • Too much risk per trade. High returns almost always mean deep drawdowns. Ask what percent of the deposit is at risk in a single trade.
  • Scammers. Promises of "guaranteed income", nice screenshots without a verifiable account, requests to send money to someone's personal account — these are the main red flags.

How to choose whom to copy: five questions

  1. Is there a public live account monitor? Not screenshots, but a link where every trade and drawdown are visible in real time.
  2. Does every trade have a stop-loss? If not, one bad trade can wipe out months of results.
  3. How does the trader get paid? The best option for you is a share of profit only: no profit, no fee. Subscriptions and fixed fees mean they earn even when you lose.
  4. Where is the money? Only in your own account with the broker. Any request to send money to the trader personally is a reason to walk away.
  5. Can you try it without risk? Normal practice is connecting to a demo account, where you see real trading without real money.
