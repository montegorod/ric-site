Gold trading robot: an EA in your terminal or copy trading — what's the difference

A trading robot is a program that opens and closes trades on its own according to a set algorithm. On Forex they are usually called Expert Advisors (EAs): they run inside the MetaTrader 4 or 5 trading platform. The appeal is clear: a robot doesn't get tired, doesn't get nervous and doesn't miss trades. There are two ways to connect a robot: install the EA directly in your own terminal, or connect to it through copy trading. Below: how a trading robot works, why there is no difference in results between these two ways, and what to look at when choosing the robot itself.

How a trading robot works

The robot watches the price and, by its own rules, decides when to enter the market, where to place the stop-loss and where to take profit. All of this happens without human involvement, around the clock while the market is open. The rules depend on the author: some robots trade trends, others pullbacks, others news.

The key thing to understand: a robot is no smarter than its algorithm and no better than the settings it was launched with. The same EA can give different results for different people — because of the broker, deposit size, settings, and how and where it runs.

Way 1: an EA directly in your terminal

The robot is installed in your MetaTrader and trades on your account itself. What this takes:

  • Installation and setup. The robot is put into the terminal with the right parameters: instrument, size, risk. Usually the robot's owner does this, or you do it yourself following their instructions.
  • 24/5 operation. The robot trades while the terminal is running, so it is kept not on a home computer but on a VPS — a remote server that runs without interruption.
  • Maintenance. Markets change, and a robot needs looking after: monitoring and updates. That is the robot owner's job.
  • A suitable broker. Spreads, execution speed and account type affect the result, especially on gold.

Pitfalls of other people's robots

  • A pretty backtest. Many EAs on the market are sold on charts of past data. Fitting an algorithm to the past is easy — and such a robot often disappoints on the live market. Look at live trading on a real account.
  • Martingale and grids. Some "profitable" EAs double the position size after a loss or open a grid of trades without a stop-loss. For a long time the chart looks perfect, and then the account is wiped out in one bad day.
  • No stop-loss. If a robot doesn't place a stop on every trade, one trade against the market can undo everything.
  • No support. Bought it, downloaded it — and from then on you're on your own with the robot.

Way 2: copy trading

The robot trades on its owner's account, and all trades are automatically copied to your broker account within a fraction of a second. What this gives you:

  • Nothing to install or configure. No VPS needed, no parameters to figure out.
  • The robot's owner maintains it. They monitor its work, the market and the risks.
  • The money stays with you. The account is in your name; the robot's owner cannot withdraw your funds.
  • Everything is visible. Every trade shows in your MetaTrader in real time.
  • You pay only on results, if the owner uses a profit-share model rather than charging for the program.

So what's the difference?

In terms of results — none. In both cases the same algorithm trades: the same trades, the same stop-losses, the same risk. An EA in your terminal opens the trade itself; with copy trading the same trade is repeated on your account within a fraction of a second. The only difference is convenience: some people feel calmer with the robot right in their own terminal, others find it easier not to install anything and just connect to copying. Choose whatever suits you.

How to choose a trading robot

Whether you install an EA or connect through copy trading, check the same things:

  1. Live trading on a real account, not just a backtest.
  2. A stop-loss on every trade.
  3. No martingale or grids — position size shouldn't multiply after a loss.
  4. Clear risk per trade — what percent of the deposit is at risk.
  5. A chance to try it — on a demo account, with no real money.
