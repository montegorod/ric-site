PAMM account, copy trading or a managed account: which to choose

If you want a professional to trade the market for you, there are usually three options: a PAMM account, a managed account and copy trading. From the outside they look alike: someone trades, you get the result. But they work differently, and the main difference is where your money sits and what you can do with it. Below is a simple comparison on the points that really matter.

What a PAMM account is

A PAMM account (Percentage Allocation Management Module) is a pooled account with a broker, where a manager trades the money of many investors at once. You deposit money into this account, and your share of the profit or loss is calculated in proportion to your contribution.

The plus: you don't need to do anything, everything happens at the broker. The minuses: the money sits in a pooled account, withdrawals are usually possible only on certain days, and you don't see individual trades in your own terminal — only the account total. Also, far from every broker offers PAMM accounts.

What a managed account is

With a managed account you hand your money to a manager or a company under an agreement, and they manage it on your behalf. It can be a large licensed asset manager, or a private trader you sent money to "for a percentage".

The plus: you hand over all the worries. The minuses: the money is physically not with you, and you depend not only on the quality of the trading but also on the reliability of the manager. With private managers this is the main source of "sent the money — the person vanished" stories. Licensed companies are safer, but the entry threshold is higher and the terms are stricter.

What copy trading is

With copy trading you have your own broker account, opened in your name. The trader's trades are automatically copied to it in real time, scaled to your deposit. The trader can only open and close trades — they cannot deposit or withdraw your money.

Comparison on the key points

Where the money is.

  • PAMM: in a pooled account at the broker.
  • Managed account: with the manager.
  • Copy trading: in your personal account, in your name.

Do you see the trades.

  • PAMM: mostly the account total.
  • Managed account: reports from the manager, usually monthly or quarterly.
  • Copy trading: every trade in your own MetaTrader terminal, in real time.

Withdrawals.

  • PAMM: often only on certain days.
  • Managed account: per the agreement, sometimes with restrictions and penalties.
  • Copy trading: at any time, by you in the broker's client area.

How you pay the manager.

  • PAMM: usually a share of profit, sometimes plus a management fee.
  • Managed account: often a fixed fee on the amount plus a share of returns — you pay even in a losing year.
  • Copy trading: depends on the trader; the best option is a share of profit only.

Can you stop.

  • PAMM: per the account rules.
  • Managed account: per the agreement.
  • Copy trading: at any time.

Which to choose

If what matters most to you is keeping your money under your control, seeing every trade and being able to withdraw or stop at any moment, copy trading fits better than the other options. It gives the same automation as a managed account, but without handing your money to anyone.

PAMM accounts and managed accounts are working tools too, especially with large licensed companies. It's just that with them you trust more: not only the trading, but the money itself.
