How much can you make with copy trading? An honest calculation and the risks

"How much can you make with copy trading?" The internet answers either "100% a month" or "nothing, it's a scam". Both extremes get in the way of a sensible decision. Below: what income really depends on, how to calculate it on your own numbers including the fee and losing months, and what expectations are reasonable.

What income depends on

Income from copy trading comes down to four things.

The trader's result. You repeat their trades, so your result in percent is roughly equal to theirs. A good month for them is a good month for you; a bad one is yours too.

Risk per trade. This is the main lever. The larger the share of the deposit at risk in a single trade, the faster the account grows in a good period and the deeper it falls in a bad one. Returns and drawdowns always come as a pair.

Deposit size. The percentages are the same, the dollars are not. 10% a month on $1,000 is $100; on $10,000 it is $1,000.

The trader's fee. Subtract it right away, or the calculation will look nice but be wrong.

A worked example

Take a $1,000 deposit and a fee of 50% of profit — as with us. The numbers are hypothetical, for the calculation only.

  • A positive month, +30%. Profit $300, fee $150, you keep $150, i.e. +15% on the deposit.
  • A negative month, −5%. We have not had such months yet, but this case has to be counted too. The account drops to $950, fee $0.
  • After the loss. No fee is charged until the account returns to its previous high — $1,000. All $50 of the recovery stays entirely with you, and we work for free during this whole period. The fee resumes only on profit above $1,000. For example, the next month is +30% on $950: the account is $1,235, profit above the high is $235, the fee is $117.50.

This rule always applies, not just to your first deposit. The "previous high" is the largest amount your account has ever reached. Say you started with $1,000, over time the account grew to $3,000, and then there was a $200 loss. Until the account is back at $3,000, there is no fee: you recover those $200 entirely for yourself, and we work for free. The fee resumes only on profit above $3,000.

Why you can't multiply one good month by twelve

The most common mistake is to see +30% in a month and conclude "so that's +360% a year". It doesn't work that way:

  • the market goes through different phases, and active months alternate with quiet ones;
  • every system has losing streaks, and after a drawdown the account needs time to get back to its previous high;
  • the higher the risk, the deeper those drawdowns and the longer the recovery.

That is why results can only be discussed honestly over a longer stretch. A week or a month can end in a loss, and that is normal. It is fairer to judge results over six months to a year.

Can you make money with copy trading?

Yes, if three conditions are met: the trader uses stop-losses and reasonable risk, you choose a risk level you can handle psychologically, and you don't switch off after the first losing week. Most often people lose money not because of copy trading as such, but because they picked the trader with the highest returns and no stops, or came in at the worst possible moment.

How to choose your risk level

Ask yourself a simple question: what loss on a single trade can I live through calmly, without hitting "stop" in a panic? 3% of the deposit? 7%? 15%? The answer matters more than any return figure in an advert. If in doubt, start with less risk: slow growth you can stick with beats fast growth you abandon in the first drawdown.

  • Income depends on the trader's result, risk per trade, the deposit and the fee.
  • Calculate after fees and including losing months — don't multiply your best month by twelve.
  • High returns always come with deep drawdowns.
  • Choose a risk level you can handle, and judge results over six months to a year.
