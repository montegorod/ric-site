Copy trading vs a bank deposit and an index fund

These three are not three versions of the same thing. They are different instruments that answer different questions, and the honest comparison is not "which one pays more" but "which one fits the money you are putting in".

If someone tells you that copy trading beats a deposit on every dimension, they are describing marketing, not money.

A bank deposit: you lend, and you get a small known rate

Money in a deposit is a loan to the bank. The face value is not supposed to move, and in most countries a deposit insurance scheme covers it up to a limit set by local law — the size of that limit is worth checking where you live. In exchange for that safety, the return is small and known in advance, and the quiet risks are inflation eating the value and a bank failure beyond the insured amount.

A deposit is the right home for money you will need soon or cannot afford to lose.

An index fund: you own a slice, and you wait

An index fund buys a broad basket of companies, so you own a small piece of hundreds of businesses rather than making a bet on one. It is cheap, it is passive — nobody is making decisions for you — and it is patient: the standard horizon is a decade, not a season.

The price moves every day. Broad markets have fallen by a third or more in bad periods before, and the recovery has historically been measured in years rather than weeks. You collect dividends along the way, and you have to do nothing at all. The risk is market risk, spread across many companies instead of concentrated in one decision.

Copy trading: your own account, someone else's decisions

Here nothing is owned. Positions are opened and closed on currencies on your own account, following the trades of a manager. There is no dividend, no basket, no long-term ownership — there is a strategy being executed right now, and its result over months.

A month can close positive, flat or negative. The strategy may hold few positions at a time, so it is not diversified in the index-fund sense. The worst month depends on the market and on the strategy, and the maximum loss built into a single trade is set in advance and small — which limits the damage from one trade but does not remove the market risk, and it is not deposit insurance.

The costs are the broker's costs on every trade, plus a share of the profit to the manager, and nothing at all in a month without profit.

The comparison that actually matters

  • What you own. A claim on a bank; a piece of many companies; or a trading account in your own name with positions in it.
  • What the result depends on. An interest rate; the long-term growth of markets; the execution of one strategy over a short horizon.
  • The worst realistic case. Face value preserved, minus inflation; a deep but historically temporary fall; a losing month, and an account below its previous high for a period of time.
  • The horizon. Months to a few years; about ten years; at least six months to a year before any judgement makes sense.
  • Your involvement. None; none; none — but you will watch it more, and that is a cost of its own.
  • How you get out. At maturity; sell and settle in days; request a withdrawal from the broker.

Which one fits you

Money you cannot afford to lose, or money you need within a year, belongs in a deposit — not here. Money you will not touch for ten years and do not want to think about fits an index fund, probably better than it fits us.

Money you can leave alone for a year, whose loss would be unpleasant but not ruinous, and where you want a chance at more than a deposit pays without trading yourself — that is the only case in which a strategy like ours is worth looking at. A month here can be negative; a deposit month cannot. That is the trade, stated plainly.

What we do not promise

We do not present this as a deposit with a better rate, or as an index fund that grows faster. We do not promise a fixed percentage, a monthly income, or that any given year will be positive. A deposit protects the face value and pays little; this pays nothing in a bad month and can lose money. Anyone who removes that trade from the description is selling something else.
